clinical-intelligence

Technical assignment Senior Backend & Platform Engineer Rutik Pol Download PDF

Architecture, retrieval and evidence provenance for a physician-facing platform, a worked trace of one real query, and a plan for taking ownership of a system somebody else built. Clinical examples are illustrative — the architecture is the subject.

The volumes are a misdirection

20,000 queries a day is 0.23 QPS — three warm pods. Ten million documents is ~300M chunks, past every measured pgvector ceiling. Sizing the API for the query count is the mistake the brief invites.

The model never writes the number

The LLM emits typed, cited statements. A deterministic gate checks every dose and threshold appears literally in its cited span. Composition is code. The model is an extractor, not an oracle.

Silence is not a recommendation

"No guideline addresses this", "guidelines recommend against", and "retrieval failed" are three different states and must never render alike. Conflating them is the failure that reaches a patient.

Monograph 01 · Platform

End-to-End Architecture

Read it in three moves: left of the gate the system is reducing, at the gate it is verifying, right of the gate it is deterministic.

  • Ingestion and query share nothing but the store. Ingestion is restartable batch on spot and may fall a day behind unnoticed; query is synchronous and budgeted. Coupling turns a parsing backlog into a latency incident.
  • No third party on the request path. Workers poll sources and model APIs on their own schedule and write; the API only reads. A throttled upstream degrades freshness, which the response reports.
  • Three deployables, not fifteen. API, workers, ingestion. At 0.23 QPS, service decomposition buys nothing and costs distributed transactions.
  • Object store is the source of truth. Versioned, Object Lock on. Every derived store — sections, chunks, vectors — is rebuildable rather than precious.
  • SQS, not a streaming platform. Ingestion is a queue of independent jobs. It becomes a real broker only when three or more consumers need replay, not for throughput.
  • Scaling order. Workers horizontally first (stateless), then Postgres read replicas, then the vector index moves out. Not the reverse.
Monograph 02 · Corpus

Ingestion, Two Clocks & Versioning

You can always recover when a guideline was published. You can never recover when you learned it. One column now, or an honest audit trail never.

  • Two clocks on every row. effective_time (in force) and ingest_time (we learned it). status='current' is a materialised view over them, not a flag anyone maintains.
  • Bitemporal constraint, range types not an extension. EXCLUDE USING gist (work_key WITH =, effective_range WITH &&, asserted_range WITH &&). Append-only — a revision is a new row, never an UPDATE.
  • Snapshot at ingest. Publishers silently revise. Without it a citation resolves against text that did not exist at the timestamp it claims.
  • Tables are a patient-safety surface. A dosing table is where the instruction lives; a mis-parsed cell turns an eGFR threshold into a dose. Low-confidence tables are quarantined, not indexed.
  • No summarisation before indexing. The gate verifies verbatim spans and a summary leaves nothing to check. Contextual retrieval prepends a blurb, never replaces the chunk — roughly 5.7% → 3.7% top-20 failure, ~2.9% with contextual BM25.
  • Not everything is chunked. A recommendation is not local: the population it applies to is often three paragraphs above the dose. Split on structure, never fixed tokens, and store char_offset back into the snapshot.
  • Dedup at two levels. Exact by hash and identifier (DOI, PMID); near-duplicate by MinHash. The same guideline arrives as publisher PDF, PubMed XML and society HTML — keep alternates as manifestations, since the PDF is what a physician wants opened at the cited page.

Multimodal: fuse at the statement, never at the tensor

Monograph 03 · Retrieval

Hybrid Search — and the Three Jobs That Are Not Search

Only one of the four retrieval jobs is a search problem. Solving the other three with vectors is the most expensive mistake available here.

  • Hybrid is insurance against disjoint failures. Dense collapses on SGLT2, NT-proBNP, eGFR <30, ICD codes — exactly what physicians type. BM25 cannot connect "kidney impairment" to "renal insufficiency".
  • RRF (k=60), not weighted blending. BM25 and cosine are incomparable scales; any fixed weight silently stops being right as the corpus grows. Tune only once accept/reject supplies labels.
  • Filters are not post-processing. Tenant, effective status, specialty, population. Adult dosing for a paediatric question is a correctness failure, not a relevance one.
  • Filtered ANN is the bad case here, not the edge case. One broad filter drops recall to ~90.8%; an AND over two broad values to ~39.7%. Every query carries four filters at once.
  • At ~300M chunks pgvector is not a close call. The measured ceiling is ~10M vectors provisioned properly, ~3M if not. My earlier instinct said 50M chunks — the evidence puts it an order of magnitude lower, so this is a when, not an if.
  • Mitigations that buy runway: halfvec, shared_buffers above the index size, dimension truncation (512 dims retains ~94–96% of NDCG@10), and pgvector 0.8+ iterative index scans.

Monograph 04 · Generation

RAG, and Where Agents Are Forced to Stop

The criterion is not "is this complex" but "is the step count knowable in advance". Almost all of this pipeline's steps are.

  • Two jobs genuinely warrant agency. Hunting corroborating primary evidence, and resolving a contradiction between guideline bodies. Both get a hard step budget and a wall-clock cap.
  • Fan out by lens, not by volume. Three agents asking the same question produce correlated agreement that reads as confirmation. Three with different remits produce information.
  • Read or search tools only. No write, no send — that breaks the injection chain even inside the agentic component.

Monograph 05 · Worked example

One Query, Traced End to End

"How should treatment differ for a patient with heart failure, CKD and type 2 diabetes according to current guidelines?" — every architectural claim above, exercised on one request.

  1. Understand — small model, ~600 tokens Conditions normalised to codes so filters can apply. Population matters: paediatric guidance would change every downstream retrieval. 
    {"conditions": ["I50.9 heart failure", "N18.3 CKD stage 3",
                "E11.9 type 2 diabetes"],
 "population": "adult", "intent": "treatment_modification",
 "needs_decomposition": true}
  2. Plan — bounded, from a fixed taxonomy Five sub-queries, hard cap six. The model chooses which types apply and how to phrase them; it cannot invent a new type. That is the difference between a bounded planner and an agent. 
    per_condition   × 3   first-line therapy for each condition
interaction     × 1   HF + CKD — agents indicated in both
dose_adjustment × 1   renal dosing at eGFR 30-59
  3. Retrieve — five parallel hybrid searches Each filtered before ranking. 50 BM25 + 50 dense per sub-query, fused by RRF, reranked to 8. BM25 is what actually finds SGLT2 and eGFR; dense is what connects "renal insufficiency" to the physician's "kidney disease". 
    filter: tenant_id=t_44  status=current
        population=adult  as_of=now()
returns: 40 passages → RRF → rerank → 8 kept
         best score 0.81   floor 0.35   → proceed
  4. Assemble — grouped, graded, budgeted Deduplicated by primary_source_id, not by document — two guidelines citing the same trial count once. Ordered by evidence grade, capped at ~4k tokens, every passage carrying its ID.
  5. Generate — typed statements, never prose The model's entire output is a list of claims with the passage IDs each came from, plus any numbers it asserted. It is not writing the answer; it is proposing evidence-linked assertions. 
    {"claim": "Metformin requires dose reduction below eGFR 45",
 "passages": ["p_9110"], "status": "recommended",
 "numbers": ["45"]}
  6. Gate — the deterministic referee Passage IDs must be in the closed set. Every number must appear verbatim in its cited span, resolved in the snapshot. One statement cited a threshold its span did not contain and was dropped — silently to the physician, loudly to Gold-A. 
    p_9110 span 4821:4977 contains "45"    ✓ accept
p_7744 span 1200:1310 asserts "40"    ✗ not in span
       → rejected, logged, never shown
  7. Compose — code, not a model Grouped by condition, conflicts shown side by side with issuing body and date, gaps stated explicitly. No second generation pass, so nothing drifts from what the gate approved.
What the answer says — and the most valuable line in it
  • Agreement, cited: one drug class is indicated across all three conditions, with three independent primary sources behind it — the strongest form of support the system can express.
  • A conflict, surfaced not resolved: two bodies differ on initiation thresholds. Both are shown, attributed by issuing body, year and evidence grade, with the axis of disagreement named. The system does not pick.
  • A gap, stated plainly: not_addressed — no current guideline covers all three conditions simultaneously. Everything above is assembled from pairwise guidance.
  • Why that last line matters most. It is the honest boundary of the evidence, and it is exactly what a fully generative system would smooth over. A physician can act on "no guideline covers this triad"; they cannot act safely on a confident synthesis that hides the same fact.
Monograph 06 · Evidence

Provenance, the Gate & the Meaning of Silence

A check that cannot fail is worse than no check, because it reports as passing.

Absence has four meanings and they must never render alike
  • recommended — a guideline affirmatively says to do it.
  • recommended_against — a guideline explicitly advises against it. This is evidence.
  • not_addressed — no current guideline covers this. This is not evidence of anything, and it is the common case for multi-morbid patients.
  • degraded — parsing or retrieval failed. We do not know, and this must never be shown as either of the two above.

An unmeasured axis widens the confidence interval rather than narrowing it. A system that answers confidently because it retrieved nothing has inverted the meaning of its own silence.

  • Corroboration counts primary sources, not documents. Three reviews citing one trial are one piece of evidence repeated cheaply — and text similarity returns exactly those copies first. Without primary_source_id, confidence rises with citation volume, which is backwards.
  • Every rejection is a free label. Mechanical, immediate, zero cost. This is the cheapest label source the architecture produces.
  • Insufficient evidence → abstain. A designed output, not an error path. Abstention rate is tracked; a rise on a topic signals a corpus gap and routes to content, not to on-call.
  • Conflicting evidence → surface, never resolve. Disagreement between bodies is a real state of medical evidence. Silently picking a winner is the worst behaviour available because it is invisible.
Monograph 07 · Platform

Core Backend & Multi-Tenancy

Row-level security handles the relational data. The vector index is the part it does not cover, and that is where isolation is actually decided.

  • Public literature is shared. The guideline is identical for every customer; duplicating ~300M chunks per tenant is indefensible and multiplies every re-embedding job by the customer count.
  • Private content is physically separate. Not a filter column. For data whose leakage would end the company, separation beats a predicate one code path can forget.
  • One retrieval function applies the tenant predicate, with a test asserting a cross-tenant query returns zero rows.
  • PgBouncer in transaction mode breaks session-scoped RLS. app.tenant_id set per connection leaks across pooled sessions — it must be SET LOCAL inside the request transaction.
  • OIDC and SAML per tenant. Hospitals arrive with Entra ID or Okta. SCIM for provisioning and, more importantly, deprovisioning.
  • Roles: physician, librarian, tenant admin, auditor. Enforced at the service boundary, never in the UI.
  • Append-only audit log in its own table with no update or delete grant, carrying the lineage quartet. The only thing that makes an answer from six weeks ago explainable.
  • No conversational memory store. The evidence table is already durable and bitemporal; a vector "memory" is a second unversioned copy of facts that already have point-in-time semantics.
  • Prompt injection is a real ingestion risk. Third-party PDFs are attacker-influenceable. Extraction is structured-output-only, no tools.
Monograph 08 · Scale

Cloud, Bottlenecks & Four Planes of Monitoring

Sub-second reads are a database index, not an achievement. The SLO that matters is that a physician can distinguish "no guidance exists" from "our pipeline stalled".

StatedDerivedConsequence
20,000 queries / day0.23 QPS · 2–4 peakThree warm pods. Serving capacity is not a design constraint — sizing for it is the misdirection.
100,000 physicians~5,000 weekly activeA user-table problem, not a load problem.
10,000,000 documents~300M chunks · ~1.2 TB fp32The actual scale problem. Sets engine, memory and quantization.
Concurrent ingestion~42 h full re-embedAn embedding upgrade is planned dual-write work, not a background task.
  • Bottlenecks in the order they bite: PDF and table parsing throughput; filtered-ANN recall at 300M vectors; LLM time-to-first-token; cross-encoder rerank (200–600 ms CPU); Postgres connections — async workers exhaust the pool long before the CPU.
  • EKS, not serverless, for the pipeline. Stages are long-running in a way Lambda's execution ceiling and cold starts fight. Serverless is right for the event glue.
  • KEDA on queue depth, not CPU. Queue depth is what correlates with ingestion backlog.
  • Ingestion runs on spot. Idempotent, checkpointed, restartable — close to an ideal spot workload.
  • Monitoring is four planes — trace (vendor gives you this), plus data, quality and cost (you build these). Quality means gate rejection rate, recall@k, abstention rate by topic, calibration.
  • The lineage no vendor supplies is trace → statement → answer → physician verdict six weeks later. Without trace_id on the statement row, "answers got worse in March" is unanswerable.
  • Freshness honesty. 100% of responses carry the effective dates of their evidence. Zero silent staleness.
  • Continuous ablation. Monthly, pull each component and measure both sides. The marginal-value-per-dollar table doubles as the documented degradation order when a budget governor fires — deciding during the incident is how you shed the wrong thing.
  • DR asymmetry. Postgres restores in minutes; the index takes days, because the expensive work is parsing and embedding. RPO/RTO differ by data class, and a backup is real only once a restore has been performed.
Monograph 09 · Economics

AI Cost & Performance

Self-hosting is a pricing-floor problem, not a utilisation problem. What forces a dedicated deployment is capacity or compliance — almost never cost.

  • 1 · Tighten context. Rerank hard to 6–8 passages rather than 20. Halves input cost, cuts TTFT, and improves quality — a model given twenty passages attends worse than one given eight.
  • 2 · Route by stage. Understanding, planning and validation do not need a frontier model. Most calls by count, a small share of spend.
  • 3 · Cache retrieval, not answers. Query-to-embedding and reranker results are exact-match and safe.
  • 4 · Stream. Perceived latency is TTFT, not total.
  • 5 · Move non-interactive work off the request path. Batch reviews are queued jobs with a notification.
  • Deferred — self-hosting. A GPU at 100% utilisation is roughly an order of magnitude above hosted per-token rates for the small tier. "We'll fill the GPU" does not fix a pricing floor. Trigger: an air-gapped or residency contract, or hosted rate limits becoming the constraint.
  • Deferred — fine-tuned embeddings. Unmeasurable without an eval set. Trigger: golden set exists and recall is the demonstrated bottleneck.
  • Deferred — cascade routing, prompt compression, GPU reranking. Speculative without production traces; CPU rerank is comfortable to ~20 QPS.
One optimisation I would refuse outright

No semantic caching of final answers. Two clinically distinct questions can sit close enough in embedding space to register as a hit — a paediatric and an adult dosing question, or the same question with and without a contraindication. Serving one for the other is a patient-safety failure disguised as a latency improvement. Exact-match on a normalised query, scoped to corpus version, is the safe form and where I would stop.

Monograph 10 · Takeover

Ownership from the External Partner

Development cannot stop, so month one buys measurement and changes nothing but security.

The golden set is three sets, and the cheapest matters most
  • Gold-A — grounding. Does the statement reflect its cited passage? Needs no clinical knowledge, the gate adjudicates much of it mechanically, and most real defects live here.
  • Gold-B — retrieval. Was the right evidence found at all?
  • Gold-C — clinical correctness. Needs physicians, stays small, and is a falsification set — enough to reject a change, never enough to fit one.
  • Two ordering rules: dedup before splitting, or the same guideline lands in both halves; and split by time.
  • What never learns: the gate, the two clocks, the abstention threshold. A referee that learns can learn to pass what it should fail, and then every metric improves while the system gets worse.

Five areas audited first

  1. Tenant isolation and PHI Is the vector search filtered by tenant or only the SQL? Is RLS on, and does pooling defeat it? Does any log, trace or prompt carry PHI? Is every processor covered by a BAA?
  2. Is there a grounding check, and can it fail? Look specifically for a verifier that cannot return false — a guard short-circuited to True, a threshold at zero, a check inside a bare except. The most common way a safety control is present in code, cited in docs, and absent in effect.
  3. Durability and the rebuild path Are raw sources retained immutably, can the index be rebuilt, has anyone tried? If the vector store holds the only parsed copy, that is a company-ending single point no backup schedule addresses.
  4. Retrieval quality — the silent risk Most likely there is no eval set, so quality is unknown rather than good. Read the actual retrieved passages for fifty real queries.
  5. Secrets, IAM, deploy and rollback Credentials in the repo, over-broad roles, public endpoints. And can we deploy without the partner, is there a rollback, are migrations reversible?

Three decisions with no single correct answer

1 · pgvector, or a dedicated engine — and when

Not whether — the measured ceilings settle that at 300M chunks — but when to spend the migration. Staying costs recall degrading invisibly under exactly the multi-filter queries this product issues; moving early costs a second source of truth before there is a corpus needing one.

Position nowStart on pgvector, retrieval behind one interface, recall@20 measured continuously under production filters. Migrate on the measurement, not the calendar. I would rather be six weeks late with a number than six months early on a hunch.
2 · How much of the answer the model may write

Fully generative reads better and handles the long tail. Extract-then-compose is auditable, testable and lets the gate check every number, at the cost of a stiffer answer and real work in the composition layer.

Position nowExtract-then-compose — the only version where "which evidence produced this sentence" has an answer that is not itself a model output. The decision I would most want to revisit with physicians in the room: if the composed answer reads as unusable to a clinician, the constraint has to move, and I would rather learn that in week three than defend it for a year.
3 · Shared corpus, or per-tenant isolation

Shared costs a fraction of the storage and embedding, one re-embedding job, one quality bar — but every isolation bug is a cross-tenant bug. Per-tenant is trivially auditable and easy to sell into a security review, at hundreds of times the index cost and a fleet operation per model upgrade.

Position nowHybrid, split by data class. Public literature in one shared corpus; tenant-private protocols in a physically separate collection, not a filter column. Full isolation stays a priced enterprise tier, so the answer to a demanding security review is commercial rather than architectural.

And what I would deliberately not build: streaming infrastructure, a dedicated vector database on day one, a feature store, a fine-tuned extractor, multi-agent debate, conversational memory, or self-hosted inference. Every one has a stated trigger above. Adopting them earlier buys operational surface for problems this system does not have, which is the most common way a small platform team stops shipping.
